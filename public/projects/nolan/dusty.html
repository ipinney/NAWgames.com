DUSTY

A little robot that lives on the table, sweeps up crumbs, and stops itself right at the edge instead of driving off.

⏰ Due Monday, November 16

The idea

What it does

A robot vacuum can't work on a table, because it would drive straight off the side. Dusty is built for exactly that job.

🧹

Sweeps

A spinning brush at the front flicks crumbs up into a little tray, the same way a dustpan works.

👀

Sees the edge

Two invisible infrared beams point down at the table. When the bounce stops coming back, the table is gone.

🛑

Stops in time

It backs up, spins a random amount, and drives off in a new direction. It has 20 mm to stop and only needs 8.

The experiment

The question I am answering

How fast can a robot drive and still stop before it falls off the table?

Going faster means less time to react. That is true for elevators, for cars that brake by themselves, and for my robot. A robot alone is just a cool thing. A robot plus a graph is a science project.

What I change
Motor speed. Four settings, from slow to full power.
What I measure
How many millimeters it takes to stop, and how many times out of 20 it falls.
What stays the same
Same table, same batteries, same sensor height, same running start.
My prediction
At full speed it needs about 20 mm to stop, and it only has 20 mm. So it goes over.

How it works

The sensor gets there first

The sensor sticks out 20 mm in front of the wheels. That head start is the whole trick. By the time the wheels reach the edge, the robot already stopped.

Watch out

Things that will probably go wrong

Writing these down now, so that when they happen it looks like I planned for them. Which I did.

A dark table looks like a cliff

The sensors work by bouncing invisible light off the table. Black placemats swallow the light, so the robot thinks the table ended and refuses to move.

Fix: press A to calibrate it on whatever table it is sitting on.

Shiny tables bounce light sideways

Glass is the worst case. The light hits it like a mirror and goes off at an angle instead of coming back.

Fix: test on glass early and just say honestly that it does not work there.

The brush throws crumbs on the sensors

A spinning brush sprays stuff everywhere, and one crumb sitting on a sensor blinds it completely.

Fix: put the sensors in front of the brush, never behind it.

Batteries get weak at the fair

After a few hours on the display table the motors slow down.

Fix: none needed. Slower means it stops sooner, so it actually gets safer.
