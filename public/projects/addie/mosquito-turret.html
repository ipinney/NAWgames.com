Requirements

The turret must do all of these. Every design choice gets checked against this list.

  • It shoots a laser.Decided Sep 15. Real mosquito turrets use lasers, so ours does too.
  • It finds and aims at the mosquito by itself.No hands on the controls. A manual mode with the micro:bit buttons is a bonus.
  • It knows when it hit.A light sensor inside the mosquito feels the laser and sends a "hit" message by radio. The turret keeps score.
  • The mosquito moves.A motor swings it back and forth, so the turret has to track it.
  • Class 2 laser, under 1 milliwatt, with the maker's label on it.The weakest visible laser class. Science fair rules require a manufacturer label so the power can be checked.
  • Mounted, never handheld, and the beam cannot leave the box.The turret only turns left and right, at mosquito height, inside a walled arena that is taller than the beam. It cannot point up at a face.
  • The laser only turns on when it is locked on a mosquito, for one second at a time.Plus an arm switch that has to be on before it can fire at all.
  • The best moment happens in the first 30 seconds of the demo.
  • It has a real experiment with numbers.Question, hypothesis, repeated trials, a graph.
  • Addie does the coding, the mosquitoes, and the testing.MakeCode blocks. Dad does the 3D printing and wiring.
  • Parts cost about $140 or less.

The problem

Houston has a lot of mosquitoes. They bite, they itch, and some carry diseases. Scientists have built machines that shoot them out of the air with lasers.

Our version: a real laser turret uses a laser strong enough to hurt a bug, which is only safe for grown-up scientists. Addie's turret does the same three jobs (find, aim, fire) with a laser so weak it can only tag the mosquito. A sensor inside the mosquito proves the hit.

How Design A works

Looking down from above. Everything lives inside a walled arena, and the beam stays below the top of the walls.

  1. See. The AI camera (HuskyLens) learns the mosquito when Addie points it at it and presses one button. It draws a box around it on its screen.
  2. Turn. Mosquito left of center: the servo turns the turret left. Right of center: turn right.
  3. Lock on. When the mosquito is in the middle, the micro:bit shows a target and beeps.
  4. Fire. If the arm switch is on, the laser turns on for one second.
  5. Hit. The red dot lands on the mosquito's white body and it glows. The light sensor inside jumps, the arena micro:bit radios "HIT", and the turret adds a point and plays a sound.

The experiment

Real mosquito turrets have to be fast. This tests how speed changes our turret's aim.

Question: Does a faster mosquito get hit less often by the laser turret?

Hypothesis

If the mosquito moves faster, then the turret will get   hits, because  .

Variables

  • Change: swing speed (slow, medium, fast), set in Addie's arena code
  • Measure: hits in 30 seconds
  • Keep the same: same turret, same mosquito, same spot, same room lights

How to test

  1. Set the arm to slow. Press start. The turret hunts for 30 seconds by itself.
  2. Write down the score. Reset. Do it 5 times.
  3. Do the same at medium and fast. That is 15 runs.
  4. Find the average for each speed and make a bar graph.
  5. Bonus: also time how long the first hit takes. That is our version of the Photonic Fence's 0.1 seconds.

Laser safety

The science fair's rules come first. These are the rules the big international fair (ISEF) uses, which many school fairs copy. Our school may be stricter, so we ask the teacher before ordering.

What ISEF says

  • Class 1, 2, 3A, and 3R lasers are allowed "to be used responsibly".
  • Lasers must carry the maker's label showing their power.
  • Handheld lasers are not allowed.
  • No beams through magnifiers or telescopes.
  • Moving parts with pinch points must be covered.
  • Unsafe lasers get taken away with no warning.

What we do

  • Class 2, the lowest visible class, under 1 mW, labeled
  • Bolted to the turret, never held
  • Turns left and right only; the beam stays 3 inches off the floor inside 8-inch walls
  • Matte black back wall, no mirrors or shiny parts in the arena
  • Fires only when locked on, one second at a time, only with the arm switch on
  • Nobody puts their face at arena height while it is armed
  • Servo gears covered
