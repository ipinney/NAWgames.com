The problem

Houston has a lot of mosquitoes. They bite, they itch, and some carry diseases. Bug spray works, but you have to put it on your skin.

1 to 1.5 mph
How fast a mosquito flies. Slower than you walk.
15 mph
How fast the wind from a regular house fan blows.
Only girls
Only female mosquitoes bite. Males drink flower nectar.
35 meters
How far away a mosquito can smell the carbon dioxide you breathe out.
174
Kinds of mosquitoes in the United States. About 2,700 in the world.
Under 0.1 s
How fast a real grown-up mosquito turret (the Photonic Fence) finds and zaps a mosquito.
The big idea: mosquitoes weigh a few milligrams and fly slower than a walk. Scientists at Cornell found that wind is one of the two best ways to stop them. So this turret does not shoot bullets. It shoots air.

Three turrets made from the three "yes" ideas. All three use the same brain: a micro:bit (the same kind Nolan uses in Dusty) and an AI camera that can learn what a mosquito looks like.

Why A is the favorite: judges can hold the mosquito and feel the blast, the turret really moves and aims, and the experiment is easy to repeat many times. Design B can be a bonus: a yeast bottle in the backyard is a fun weekend test either way.

How Design A works

Side view. The whole cannon sits on a lazy susan so one small servo can spin it left and right.

  1. See. The AI camera (HuskyLens) is taught what the mosquito looks like by pointing at it and pressing one button. It draws a box around it on its little screen.
  2. Turn. If the mosquito is left of center, the micro:bit tells the servo to turn the turret left. Right of center, turn right.
  3. Lock on. When the mosquito stays in the middle for half a second, the micro:bit shows a target on its lights and beeps.
  4. Fire. A small motor turns a snail-shaped cam. The cam pulls the rubber skin back, then it slips off the edge and the skin snaps forward. That pushes a doughnut of air out of the hole.
  5. Score. Each hit adds a point on the micro:bit. The judge tries to escape.

The experiment

A turret is an invention. A science fair wants a test with numbers. This one uses the cannon itself.

Question: Does the size of the hole change how far the air cannon can knock down a mosquito?

Hypothesis

If the hole is  , then the air ring will knock the mosquito down from farther away, because  .

Variables

  • Change: hole size (3 snap-on caps: small 20 mm, medium 30 mm, large 40 mm)
  • Measure: hits out of 5 at each distance
  • Keep the same: same cannon, same skin, same paper mosquito, same table, fan and AC off

How to test

  1. Fold a paper mosquito into a little tent so it stands up.
  2. Tape a line on the floor every 2 feet: 2, 4, 6, 8 feet.
  3. Put on the small cap. Stand the mosquito at 2 feet. Fire 5 times. Count how many knock it over.
  4. Move to 4, 6, then 8 feet. Then do it all again with the medium and large caps.
  5. That is 60 shots. Make a bar graph: one bar for each cap at each distance.

Where the facts came from

  • American Mosquito Control Association, FAQs: flight speed, only females bite, CO2 from 35 m, 174 US species, 2,700 worldwide.
  • Cornell, Evidence-Based Living: wind and DEET were the two most effective ways to avoid mosquitoes; house fan about 15 mph.
  • ScienceAlert, Photonic Fence: finds bugs by wingbeat, size and flight, fires in under 100 milliseconds.
  • Yeast-generated CO2 for mosquito trapping: the yeast-and-sugar bait in Design B.
  • Hackster: pan and tilt tracking with HuskyLens and micro:bit: proof the camera-plus-servo idea works.
