Can a laser kill a mosquito?

Yes, but not a safe one. Scientists measured exactly how much laser energy it takes, and the answer is a great fact for the board.

7 J/cm²
Energy per spot that killed 90% of mosquitoes (blue or green laser, 1/40 of a second flash).
~14 watts
Laser power that takes, for a 2.5 mm spot in that 1/40 of a second.
0.001 watts
Our Class 2 laser.
14,000×
How much weaker ours is than a mosquito-killing flash.

Why we don't use a stronger laser

  • A 14 watt laser is Class 4, the most dangerous class. It can blind an eye instantly, even from a reflection off a shiny surface, and it can start fires.
  • Science fair rules (ISEF) allow only Class 1, 2, 3A, and 3R. Class 3B and Class 4 are not on the list.
  • Going up to the strongest allowed class (5 mW) still leaves us about 2,800 times too weak. It adds risk and does not kill anything.
  • Holding our 1 mW beam on one spot does not add up either: the scientists found longer, weaker flashes kill less, because the heat leaks away.

How the real Photonic Fence stays safe

  • It is built by grown-up laser scientists and works 30 meters away.
  • It will not fire if a person or animal is in the zone.
  • The newer systems use a laser color that is safer for the retina.

Addie's turret does the same finding, tracking, and hit-proving as the real one. The only thing it leaves out is the power, on purpose. That choice goes on the board as part of the science.

Math: 7.1 J/cm² × area of a 2.5 mm spot (0.049 cm²) = 0.35 J. Delivered in 0.025 s = 14 W. 14 W / 0.001 W = 14,000. Source: Keller et al., Scientific Reports, 2020.

How Design A works

Side view. The mosquito flies inside the flight zone, a black box open toward the turret. The beam can only end on the black back wall.

  1. Train. Addie points the camera at the mosquito, holds the learn button, and slowly turns the mosquito so the camera learns it from every side and distance. Then she saves it.
  2. Track. The camera reports where the mosquito is on its screen (320 by 240 dots). Too far left, the pan servo turns left. Too high, the tilt servo tips up, but never past the printed stop.
  3. Lock on. When the mosquito is inside a small box in the middle of the screen, the micro:bit shows a target and beeps.
  4. Fire. If the arm switch is on, the laser flashes for a quarter of a second.
  5. Prove it. The laser dot lands on the mosquito's white body and it glows. The light sensor inside jumps. The wand micro:bit flashes the mosquito's red eyes, buzzes, shows an X, and radios "HIT". The turret adds a point.

The experiment

A mosquito that swings farther moves faster. The pendulum hook makes each swing the same.

Question: Does a faster mosquito get hit less often by the laser turret?

Hypothesis

If the mosquito swings faster, then the turret will get   hits, because  .

Variables

  • Change: how far back the mosquito is pulled before letting go (10, 20, or 30 degrees on the hook scale)
  • Measure: hits counted by the wand in 30 seconds
  • Keep the same: same turret, mosquito, line length, spot, and room lights

How to test

  1. Hang the wand on the hook. Pull the mosquito back to 10 degrees and let go.
  2. Press start. The turret hunts for 30 seconds. Write down the hits.
  3. Do it 5 times, then do the same at 20 and 30 degrees. That is 15 runs.
  4. Find the average for each and make a bar graph.
  5. Bonus: the wand also times how long the first hit took.

Laser safety

These are the rules the big international fair (ISEF) uses, which many school fairs copy. Our school may be stricter, so we ask the teacher before ordering.

What ISEF says

  • Class 1, 2, 3A, and 3R lasers are allowed "to be used responsibly".
  • Lasers must carry the maker's label showing their power.
  • Handheld lasers are not allowed.
  • No beams through magnifiers or telescopes.
  • Moving parts with pinch points must be covered.
  • Unsafe lasers get taken away with no warning.

What we do

  • Class 2, the lowest visible class, under 1 mW, labeled
  • Bolted into the printed head, never held
  • Printed tilt stop plus a code limit: it cannot aim above the flight zone
  • Flight zone has a black top and back; no mirrors or shiny parts inside
  • Short flashes only when locked on, only with the arm switch on
  • The wand holder stands beside the turret, never inside the flight zone or in front of it
  • Servo gears covered

Where the facts came from

  • Keller et al., Optical tracking and laser-induced mortality of insects during flight, Scientific Reports (2020): lethal doses of about 7 J/cm² at 445 and 532 nm with a 2.5 mm beam and 25 ms pulse; longer pulses killed less.
  • An optical system to detect, surveil, and kill flying insect vectors, Scientific Reports (2024): works at 30 m; will not fire when people or animals enter the zone.
  • ScienceAlert, Photonic Fence: finds bugs by wingbeat, size and flight; fires in under 100 milliseconds.
  • Society for Science, ISEF Display and Safety Rules: laser classes, labels, no handheld lasers, pinch points.
  • Quarton VLM-650-03 manual: the LPT version is under 1 mW, Class 2, with a safety label.
  • DFRobot: HuskyLens object tracking with micro:bit: hold the learn button to learn an object at different angles and distances; the micro:bit gets its X and Y position.
  • American Mosquito Control Association, FAQs: flight speed, only females bite, CO2 from 35 m, 174 US species, 2,700 worldwide.
