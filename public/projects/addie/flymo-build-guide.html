Read this part first

  • Props spin thousands of times a minute. The drone only flies with its guard on, inside the net. Hands out while the props turn.
  • Takeoff only from the pad. Nobody stands over the net. A long press on GO lands it right away.
  • LiPo batteries are a grown-up job. Charge on a hard surface with a grown-up nearby. Never crush, pierce, or short them.
  • Unplug the battery after every session. The mosquito's radio stays on while the battery is plugged in.
  • A puffy, hot, or smelly battery is done. A grown-up moves it outside on a hard surface and recycles it.
  • Check the battery plug. Battery brands do not all wire the plug the same way. Match + to + before the first plug-in.
  • Never look into the MS-2000 laser. The drone's own height laser is invisible and eye safe (Class 1).
  • Soldering is a grown-up job. Eye protection on. The iron is hotter than an oven.

Part A · Dec 1 to 6

Check and print

Find surprises on day one, fly the plain drone, then print the small test first.

1

Unbox, check, and measure

Day 1 · about 1 hour · grown-up

Same as Dusty: a wrong or missing part gets found now, not on build day. Four numbers in the 3D model are guesses until we measure.

  1. Check every part against the shopping list. Write what arrived and what did not.The drone and module ship from India, so they may come separately.
  2. Measure with calipers: how far the motor sticks out under the board, how high the props sit, the battery size, and how far the module hangs down once it is plugged in.Write all four in the journal. They go into the 3D model.
  3. Weigh the drone, one battery, and the module.The weight table uses 45, 16 and 8 g until now.
  4. Look at the battery plug and the + and - marks on the board next to it. Do they match?If not, a grown-up swaps the two pins in the plug. Never force it.
Do not plug in a battery yet until the polarity check is done.

Checkpoint

Every part checked in, four measurements and three weights written down.

Journal: the date, what arrived, the numbers you measured, and anything different from the plan.

2

First flight with the phone app

Day 2 · 30 minutes · grown-up

Prove the drone works before anything gets changed. If it will not fly now, it is the drone, not our parts.

  1. Charge a battery on the drone by USB-C. The FULL light comes on when it is done.Or use the battery charger that came with the pack.
  2. Set the drone on a flat floor, plug in the battery, switch it on, and leave it still until the green light blinks fast.Slow green blinking means it is calibrating. Do not touch it.
  3. Install the LiteWing app, join the drone WiFi, and hover for 2 minutes in an open room.Low and slow. Guard is not on yet, so a grown-up flies and everyone stands back.
  4. Land, unplug the battery.

Why does it need to sit still at the start?

The motion sensor has to learn what "not moving" feels like. If it gets bumped while it learns, the drone thinks it is tipping and drifts.

Checkpoint

The plain drone hovers for 2 minutes.

3

Flash the drone and add the positioning module

Day 3 · 30 minutes · grown-up

The positioning module needs newer firmware on the drone. Firmware is the program that lives inside the drone.

  1. Open the LiteWing web flasher in Chrome, plug the drone in by USB-C, and flash the positioning module firmware.Battery unplugged while flashing.
  2. Check the drone has female headers underneath. If not, a grown-up solders four 6-pin headers on.Straight up, flush to the board.
  3. Plug the module in underneath, arrow pointing at the USB-C port, pressing evenly on both sides.No bent pins.
  4. Power up. The module's 4 lights flash once.If the drone does not start, power off and check the arrow.

Checkpoint

Module plugged in and its lights flash at startup.

4

Print the fit check

Day 3 · 10 minutes of printing

A 10 minute print tells us which motor collar size grips best before the 33 minute guard gets printed.

  1. Print plate 1, the fit check.Any color.
  2. Take one motor out of the drone and slide it into each collar: 1 notch is 7.0 mm, 2 notches 7.2 mm, 3 notches 7.4 mm.The right one grips firmly and does not split.
  3. Push the arcade button through the big hole and the light sensor into the pocket.
  4. Tell the grown-up the winning collar size. It goes into the guard model before plate 2 prints.

Part B · Dec 7 to 13

The experiment: Can it fly?

Before building more, find out how much the drone can really carry. The answer can still change the design.

6

Run the Can it fly? experiment

Dec 7 to 13 · two tests, 18 flights · grown-up

Our math says the four props push about 120 g and the full drone weighs about 87 g. The experiment checks if the math is right.

  1. Test A, the push test: strap the drone to a heavy block on a kitchen scale, zero it, and a grown-up runs the motors at 25, 50, 75 and 100% for 3 seconds each.The scale reads below zero. That number is the push in grams.
  2. Test B, the hover test: hover 30 cm off the floor until the low-battery light, with 0, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7 nickels taped to the middle. Three flights each.The laptop logs battery voltage and motor power.
  3. Fill in the data sheet and draw the two graphs.Minutes vs grams added, and push at each power level.
  4. Use the decision rules to pick: keep Rev A, go lighter, or bigger props.

Push test (Python, grown-up)

import time, cflib.crtp
from cflib.crazyflie import Crazyflie
URI = "udp://192.168.43.42"          # the LiteWing address on its own WiFi
cflib.crtp.init_drivers()
cf = Crazyflie(); cf.open_link(URI); time.sleep(1)
cf.commander.send_setpoint(0, 0, 0, 0)  # unlock
for pct in (25, 50, 75, 100):
    thrust = int(10000 + (60000 - 10000) * pct / 100)
    t0 = time.time()
    while time.time() - t0 < 3:      # keep sending, or the drone stops
        cf.commander.send_setpoint(0, 0, 0, thrust); time.sleep(0.05)
    cf.commander.send_setpoint(0, 0, 0, 0); input(f"{pct}%: read the scale, press Enter")
cf.close_link()

Drone strapped down, guard on, hands clear. LiteWing thrust runs from 10000 to 60000.

Part C · Dec 14 to 20

Teach it to fly by itself

First from the laptop, over a flight mat, in the net.

7

Make the flight mat and set up the net

About 1 hour

The floor camera sees movement by watching the floor slide by. A plain or shiny floor gives it nothing to see, and the drone drifts.

  1. Glue a printed high-contrast pattern (checkers, dots, or a newspaper page) onto poster board.Matte paper, not shiny.
  2. Pop up the bug habitat. Roll the front panel up and pin it open.The open side faces where the turret will sit, 3 feet away.
  3. Lay the mat on the habitat floor and the launch pad on top when it is printed.Until then, the drone starts on the mat.

How does a floor camera know where it is?

It takes hundreds of tiny pictures of the floor every second. When the pattern slides left in the picture, the drone is moving right, so it pushes back. That is called optical flow. A computer mouse works the same way.

Checkpoint

Net up, mat down, ready to fly.

8

Hover by itself from the laptop

About 2 evenings · grown-up

The laptop sends "stay at 40 cm, do not move" 10 times a second, and the drone uses its floor camera and range finder to obey.

  1. Install Python and the Crazyflie library (cflib) on the laptop. Join the drone WiFi.pip install cflib
  2. Guard on, battery in, drone on the mat, facing the open side of the net.
  3. Run the hover script: take off to 40 cm, hold 20 seconds, land.Measure how far it drifts with a ruler taped to the mat.
  4. Then run the pattern script: a slow square and a figure eight at 0.2 m per second.If it drifts out of the zone, the script lands it.

Hover and pattern (Python, grown-up)

import time, cflib.crtp
from cflib.crazyflie import Crazyflie
from cflib.crazyflie.syncCrazyflie import SyncCrazyflie
URI, HEIGHT, SPEED = "udp://192.168.43.42", 0.4, 0.2   # meters, meters per second
cflib.crtp.init_drivers()

def hold(cf, vx, vy, z, seconds):
    for _ in range(int(seconds * 10)):           # 10 times a second
        cf.commander.send_hover_setpoint(vx, vy, 0, z)   # yaw rate 0: window keeps facing the turret
        time.sleep(0.1)

with SyncCrazyflie(URI, cf=Crazyflie()) as scf:
    cf = scf.cf
    cf.commander.send_setpoint(0, 0, 0, 0)       # unlock
    for i in range(1, 21):                       # climb over 2 seconds
        hold(cf, 0, 0, HEIGHT * i / 20, 0.1)
    for vx, vy in [(SPEED, 0), (0, SPEED), (-SPEED, 0), (0, -SPEED)] * 3:
        hold(cf, vx, vy, HEIGHT, 2)               # 40 cm each side
    for i in range(20, 0, -1):                   # come down slowly
        hold(cf, 0, 0, HEIGHT * i / 20, 0.1)
    cf.commander.send_stop_setpoint()

Based on LiteWing's own position hold examples. The final version lives on the Pi (step 16).

Checkpoint

It takes off, flies the square inside the zone, and lands, 3 times in a row.

Journal: how far it drifted each time.

Part D · Dec 21 to Jan 3

The hit sensor

The light sensor feels the laser, and the XIAO tells the control box.

9

Wire the sensor and the XIAO

About 1 hour · grown-up

Three short wires from the sensor, two from the battery. Everything stays inside the body.

  1. Solder 3 thin wires to the light sensor: VIN, GND, OUT. Heat shrink each joint.About 6 cm long.
  2. Solder VIN to the XIAO 3V3 pin, GND to GND, OUT to A0 (also marked D0).See the wiring map.
  3. Solder two wires to the BAT+ and BAT- pads under the XIAO, and the other ends to the + and - pins on the back of the drone's battery plug.Red to +. Check twice before any battery goes in.
  4. Press the sensor into the pocket on the body's front wall, face out. XIAO flat on the floor, USB-C toward the tail slot.A dot of hot glue on each wire where it leaves the board.
Unplug the battery after every session. The XIAO is wired straight to the battery, so it stays on until the battery comes out.

Checkpoint

Nothing shorted: with the battery in, the XIAO light comes on and nothing gets warm.

10

Code the XIAO: feel the hit

About 1 evening · grown-up

The XIAO learns how bright the room is, then shouts HIT when the light jumps. It waits 1 second before it can shout again, like the MS-2000.

Hit sensor (Arduino on the XIAO, grown-up)

#include <WiFi.h>
#include <WiFiUdp.h>
const char* SSID = "LiteWing_xxxx";   // the drone WiFi name
const char* PASS = "........";        // and password, from the LiteWing guide
const int JUMP = 150;                 // how big a jump counts as a hit: set in the flashlight test
WiFiUDP udp; long base = 0; unsigned long lastHit = 0;

void setup() {
  WiFi.begin(SSID, PASS);
  while (WiFi.status() != WL_CONNECTED) delay(200);
  for (int i = 0; i < 200; i++) { base += analogRead(A0); delay(5); }
  base /= 200;                        // normal room light
}

void loop() {
  int v = analogRead(A0);
  if (v > base + JUMP && millis() - lastHit > 1000) {
    udp.beginPacket(IPAddress(192, 168, 43, 255), 5005);   // shout to everyone on the drone WiFi
    udp.print("HIT"); udp.endPacket();
    lastHit = millis();
  } else if (v < base + JUMP / 2) {
    base = (base * 31 + v) / 32;      // slowly follow the room light
  }
  delay(5);                           // 200 checks a second
}

Upload with the Arduino IDE (board: XIAO_ESP32C3). The laptop can listen on port 5005 to see the HITs.

  1. Flashlight test: flick a flashlight across the white window. The laptop shows HIT.
  2. Now the real laser (grown-up turns it on): point the MS-2000 laser at the window. HIT every time.If it misses, make JUMP smaller. If it shouts HIT with no laser, make JUMP bigger.

Checkpoint

10 laser flicks, 10 HITs, and no HIT when nothing happens for a minute.

Journal: the JUMP number you picked and why.

13

Set up the Pi

About 1 evening · grown-up

The Pi is a small Linux computer. It joins the drone WiFi like a phone would, and runs the flight script by itself when it turns on.

  1. Use Raspberry Pi Imager to put Raspberry Pi OS Lite on the microSD card. In the settings, type in the drone WiFi name and password, and turn on SSH.
  2. Boot the Pi, log in from the laptop, and install the library: pip install cflib pyserial gpiozero.
  3. Copy the flight script (step 16) to the Pi and set it to start at power-on.A small systemd service called flymo.
  4. Screw the Pi onto its four posts on the box floor.M2.5 x 6.

Wire the box

About 1 hour · grown-up

16

The flight script: press GO

About 1 evening · grown-up

One script on the Pi ties it all together. It waits for the button, flies the run, passes every HIT to the micro:bit, and lands.

flymo.py on the Pi (grown-up)

import socket, time, serial, threading
from gpiozero import Button, LED
# ... hold() and the pattern from step 8 ...
button, light = Button(17, hold_time=1.5), LED(27)
mb = serial.Serial("/dev/ttyACM0", 115200)          # the micro:bit
say = lambda word: mb.write((word + "\n").encode())
hits = socket.socket(socket.AF_INET, socket.SOCK_DGRAM); hits.bind(("", 5005))

def listen():                                        # HIT from the XIAO -> micro:bit
    while True:
        data, _ = hits.recvfrom(16)
        if data == b"HIT": say("HIT")
threading.Thread(target=listen, daemon=True).start()

while True:
    light.on(); button.wait_for_press(); light.off()
    if battery_volts() < 3.8: light.blink(); continue   # too low to fly a full run
    say("GO"); fly_run(seconds=30); say("STOP")         # button.when_held lands right away

The full file lives in the repo with the other tools.

  1. Drone on the pad, battery in, box switched on, wait for the GO light.
  2. Press GO. It takes off, flies the pattern for 30 seconds, and lands on the pad.
  3. Hold GO for 1.5 seconds during a flight: it lands right away.Practice this before anything else.

Checkpoint

Press GO, full run, lands in the cups. The emergency landing works.

Words to know

Say these like a scientist

ThrustThe push from the props.
PayloadThe extra weight a drone carries.
Thrust-to-weight ratioMost push divided by weight. Needs to be over 1 to fly.
Optical flowSeeing movement by watching a picture slide, like a computer mouse.
Time of flightMeasuring distance by timing a bounce of light.
FirmwareThe program that lives inside a device.
Feedback loopMeasure, decide, fix, repeat. Hundreds of times a second.
ProtocolThe words two machines agree to use: GO, HIT, STOP.
LiPoLithium polymer battery. Light and powerful, handle with care.
ThresholdHow big a change has to be before it counts. Our JUMP number.
Press fitParts that hold because they are a tight squeeze, with no screws.
Change orderThe written plan and approval for changing a locked design.
